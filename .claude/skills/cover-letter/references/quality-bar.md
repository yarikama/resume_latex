# Quality Bar

Distilled from the CMU Global Communication Center cover letter handout and the Rice
Center for Career Development guidelines. The source PDFs have been removed; this is
the record of what they said.

## The four body-paragraph principles

### 1. Connect past → present → future

Show how past experience makes him a candidate now, and how those qualifications
will be an asset to *this* employer's work.

> **Before** — "I graduated from CMU's Master's in Statistical Practice program.
> Before that, I was an undergraduate at the University of Vermont where I majored in
> Finance with a minor in Economics. I also worked as an academic advisor."
>
> *Restates the résumé without interpreting any skill acquired.*

> **After** — "CMU's Master's of Statistical Practice program is uniquely focused on
> professional development and practical workplace skills. The client management
> **skills I've acquired** through my coursework in consulting and communicating
> analytic results **make me a strong addition** to Walgreen's team of data analysts,
> who are committed to effectively communicating trends to internal and external
> clients."
>
> *Explains past and present experience, then explains how it benefits the future.*

### 2. Highlight the qualification, not the experience

The letter must not restate the résumé. It elaborates on what his abilities and
accomplishments *gave* him.

> **Before** — "This past year, I was a research assistant with Dr. XXX and I worked
> alongside other chemical engineers to ensure we completed our project by the
> deadline."
>
> *Never states what the applicant gained from the experience.*

> **After** — "**As a research assistant with Dr. XXX**, I worked towards the device
> implementation of semiconducting conjugated polymers and **acquired** integrated
> technical judgment and a background in modeling from first principles. **Through my
> research assistantship I have been prepared** to provide materials expertise to
> Sandia National Laboratories' customers."
>
> *States qualifications gained, then links them to future work.*

### 3. Be specific — show, don't tell

Eliminate sentences that could be written by anybody with the same degree.

> **Before** — "I was excited when I saw the job posting for a Software Development
> Engineer at Microsoft because it is a great company to work for. I believe I am a
> good candidate because of my enthusiasm, my love for computer science, and my
> knowledge of many programming languages."
>
> *Doesn't explain why the company is great to work for. Every applicant has these
> qualifications.*

> **After** — "As per our conversation at Carnegie Mellon's Technical Opportunities
> Conference, I am writing to apply for the Software Development Engineer position.
> My advisor, Professor XXXX, has a close relationship with your division and
> encouraged me to pursue this position. As a recent graduate from CMU's School of
> Computer Science, **I am an ideal candidate due to** my experience with web back-end
> development and app design, particularly with C#, Objective C, JavaScript, and PHP.
> More specifically, my experience building modern phone applications in C++ **will be
> an asset** to Microsoft's team of engineers."
>
> *Mentions contacts within the company, explains why he is ideal, shows how past
> experience benefits future work.*

### 4. One qualification per paragraph

The first or second sentence states one or two specific qualifications. Everything
after supports that single idea. Do not jump between unrelated qualifications inside
one paragraph — it leaves the reader without a unified idea of the applicant.

## Checklists

### Is the letter…

- [ ] Consistent with the résumé — same header, same font? *(The template guarantees
      this: both `\input` `content/shared/header.tex`.)*
- [ ] No more than one page?
- [ ] Free of spelling and grammar errors?
- [ ] Tailored to this specific position?

### Does the letter…

- [ ] Address a specific person or the hiring manager? (**Never** "To whom it may
      concern.")
- [ ] State why he is interested in the position *and* the organisation?
- [ ] Match his qualifications to the position and organisation?
- [ ] Give specific examples illustrating past accomplishments?
- [ ] **Use varied sentence structures instead of starting each sentence with "I"?**
- [ ] Indicate desire and availability for an interview?

### Introduction — did it…

- [ ] Name the position and how he heard about it?
- [ ] State degree, major, institution, graduation date?
- [ ] End with a strong claim previewing the 1–3 qualifications the body will cover?

### Conclusion — did it…

- [ ] Remind the reader why he is a strong match for the position and company?
- [ ] Mention enclosed documents (résumé, portfolio)?
- [ ] Request an interview or refer to future contact?
- [ ] Provide contact information?
- [ ] Thank the reader for their consideration?

## Formatting

- Concise and economical, with **ample white space** — at least 1" margins, short
  paragraphs. Recruiters may review thousands of letters and will not read a page
  that looks crowded.
- The heading carries contact information *and* creates white space, making the page
  friendlier to read.
- Structure: header → date → contact person, title, company, address → `RE:` position
  line → salutation → intro → body → conclusion → `Sincerely,` → signature → name.

## Research questions to answer before writing

From the Rice guidelines — these are inputs to step 3 of the workflow:

- To whom should the letter be addressed?
- Why does he want this job?
- What qualifications does the employer want? Identify **at least three** traits.
- How does his experience relate? List **three** achievements tied to the duties.
- Why this organisation? Find something specific about its services, product,
  mission, or vision — not that it is an "excellent company".

## What a cover letter is for

- Demonstrate he is a good fit for the position *and* the organisation.
- Convey motivation, enthusiasm, and qualifications beyond what the résumé shows.
- Showcase personality and the ability to convey ideas clearly.
