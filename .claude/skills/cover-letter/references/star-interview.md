# STAR Interview — Question Bank

Ask 6–8 targeted questions per story, **one cluster at a time**, stopping for answers
between clusters. Tag each question with the job-ad requirement that motivated it:

> `[Must-have: "Python; data pipelines"]` — When you scaled the indexing pipeline,
> what was the failure mode you were actually fixing?

Check the `star_stories.md` memory file before asking anything — several of Henry's
stories (MaiAgent agent upgrade, testing advocacy, LlamaIndex contributions, the
Google Home internship, team structure) are already documented there. Re-asking
answered questions wastes his time and signals you did not look.

## Situation

- What company/team/organisation? What do they build? What is their mission?
  (Skip the mission question for well-known companies.)
- His role/title and duration.
- Class project, internship, research, or paid work?
- Solo or team? If team: how large, and which part was his?

## Task

- What problem was he solving, or what goal was he given?
- Specific deliverable, deadline, or expected outcome?
- Constraints — technical, time, budget, organisational?

## Action

- What steps did he take, in order?
- Which tools, languages, systems, processes? Name specifics.
- Was this code, analysis, design, communication, or leadership?
- Independent contribution or did he own a piece? What exactly was his?
- How did he collaborate and communicate?

## Result

- What changed as a result?
- Quantify where possible: %, $, time, throughput, accuracy, coverage.
- Who benefited, and how many? (Omit the number when it is small or obvious —
  "helped 3 teammates" weakens rather than strengthens.)
- Any recognition, adoption, or feedback?

## Probing areas

Each of these ends in a judgement call — ask, then decide whether it earns space in a
one-page letter.

**Impact / metrics.** Before-and-after numbers. If none exist, capture the
qualitative benefit instead of leaving a vague claim. Then decide: does stating it
add force, or is it redundant with something already said?

**Scope.** Role and duration; solo or team, and team size. Is the scale or the
collaboration the more relevant fact for *this* ad?

**Reach.** Who was affected — users, customers, other engineers. Does the number
demonstrate reach, or does quoting it shrink the achievement?

**Tools and tech.** Which, and how they were applied. Name versions where it matters.

**Constraints and challenges.** What was hard, and how he got around it. Trade-offs
navigated — latency vs cost, speed vs safety, compliance limits. This is usually
where the most persuasive material hides, because it is the part no other applicant
can copy.

**Leadership and communication.** Decisions he drove, stakeholders he influenced,
people he mentored. Cross-timezone or cross-cultural collaboration. Docs, specs, or
presentations he produced.

## Verb calibration

Before writing the paragraph, pin down what he actually did:

| Use | When |
|---|---|
| designed, architected, led, owned | he made the decisions and was accountable |
| built, implemented, shipped | he did the work within someone else's design |
| contributed to, supported, assisted | he helped on a piece someone else owned |

Ask directly when it is ambiguous — the difference between "designed" and
"contributed to" is exactly what an interviewer will probe. Avoid "pioneered"
entirely unless it is literally true.
